# Drawing with Machines — Course Profile

## Key Topics

**What are the key subject topics that this course will cover?**

- Computational and generative approaches to drawing
- Rule-based art, procedurality, randomness, and algorithmic composition
- Creative coding for the generation of vector graphics
- Computer-controlled robotics for mark-making
- Computational control of line, line weight, tone, hatching, pattern, and texture
- Material experimentation with pens, inks, paints, papers, and other drawing media
- Relationships between digital processes and physical/material outcomes
- Historical and contemporary practices in generative, computational, and machine-assisted drawing
- Development of experimental, self-directed workflows combining software, hardware, and physical media

## Prerequisite Knowledge

**What prior knowledge must students have in order to be successful in this course? Do not list prerequisite courses.**

- This is an intermediate/advanced studio course, intended for arts students who have already had at least one semester of elementary programming (in any language). Students must be familiar and comfortable with computer programming fundamentals, such as iteration, conditional testing, functional abstraction, static and dynamic memory structures, and object-oriented programming.
- Students are expected to have had some prior experience with a creative coding toolkit for the arts, such as p5.js or Processing.
- This is a polyglot course, taught using a combination of JavaScript, Java, and Python. In general, students may work in their preferred programming language. However, students who are fluent in only one programming language may need to do some additional preparation.
- A strong foundation in mathematics, especially including geometry, algebra, and trigonometry, will be helpful.
- A strong foundation in markmaking, especially including drawing, painting, diagramming or drafting, will be helpful.

## Course Relevance

**How is this course relevant to the targeted student populations?**

This course is relevant to students interested in computational art, drawing, communication design, generative design, digital fabrication, creative coding, and experimental approaches to art and technology. It provides students with prior programming experience an opportunity to apply computational thinking to physical art-making, while providing students with backgrounds in drawing and design an opportunity to extend their practices through code and automation.

As an interdisciplinary studio course, *Drawing with Machines* brings together skills and perspectives from art, design, computer science, and fabrication. Students learn to treat computational drawing as a material practice, with the plotter serving as a site where algorithms, machines, physical materials, historical drawing practices, and individual artistic decisions collide.

## Course Goals

**What are the overall goals of this course that students will achieve after completing it?**

After completing this course, students will be able to:

- Create original computational and generative drawings using creative coding techniques.
- Develop programs and workflows for controlling pen plotters and other computer-controlled drawing machines.
- Apply computational techniques to control fundamental graphical properties including line, weight, tone, texture, pattern, and composition.
- Understand how the physical properties of drawing tools and materials interact with computational processes.
- Develop experimental workflows that integrate software, hardware, and traditional art materials.
- Demonstrate familiarity with significant historical and contemporary artists, artworks, and practices in generative and machine-assisted drawing.
- Develop and execute increasingly self-directed creative investigations using computational drawing.
- Document, present, and critically discuss computational artworks and their processes.

## Assessment Structure

**How will students be assessed in this course: assignments, exams, final, presentation, project, etc.?**

Students will be assessed based on their responses to approximately 10 sets of Assignments. These include 7 weekly assignment sets prior to the mid-semester break; a 2-week Experiment, with a proposal stage; a 4-week Investigation, with proposal and check-in stages; and a culminatory Portfolio Review during Finals Week.

## Learning Resources

**What resources will be available for students: web pages, learning applications, texts, case studies, etc.?**

- An extensive Github repository, containing lectures, assignments, readings, and tutorials.
- A Discord server, for posting your work, announcements, and informal communication.
- An in-class library of books.

## Extra Time Commitments

**Are there extra time commitments required outside of the regularly scheduled course meeting times?**

There are no specific extra time commitments outside of regularly posted meeting times. However, students should plan to use specialized plotting equipment outside of class time. Note that, just as with a hand-drawing, some plots can take a very long time (e.g. 10 hours) to execute.
